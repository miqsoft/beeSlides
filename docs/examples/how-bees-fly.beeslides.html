1934 · THE BUMBLEBEE
The myth
“According to the laws of aerodynamics, the bumblebee cannot fly.”
The myth was about the bumblebee, the honeybee’s heavier cousin — same physics, same mistake. (Often quoted; the exact wording was never traced.)
A zoologist and a mathematician did the sums for a rigid wing.
For wings that small, that gave far too little lift.
The mistake: insect wings flap, twist and shed vortices.

AERODYNAMICS
Where does the lift come from?
L=12ρv2SCL
ρ — density of the air
v — speed of the wing
S — wing area
CL — lift coefficient: here is the trick
A vortex at the leading edge “sticks” to the wing and lowers the pressure above it. On top come the forces of turning the wing at the end of every stroke.

BEFORE TAKE-OFF
Warm up first, then take off
Below ~10 °C: the flight muscles barely get going.
Shivering: the muscles work against each other while the wings stay still — that heats the thorax.
From ~35 °C in the thorax: take-off.
